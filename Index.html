Final Year Research Project · R26-SE-019

Transformer-Based Real-Time Bidirectional AI System for the Deaf & Hearing Community

An academic research project documenting the design, development, and evaluation of the first integrated real-time communication bridge for Sri Lankan Sign Language (SSL) — enabling bidirectional interaction between Deaf and hearing individuals, with integrated environmental safety awareness and embedded SSL learning.

Project Overview

Abstract

Effective communication between Deaf sign language users and hearing individuals remains a critical accessibility challenge in Sri Lanka, where over 300,000 Deaf and hearing-impaired individuals face profound social isolation, economic marginalization, and educational disparities due to the absence of real-time bidirectional translation between Sinhala spoken language and Sri Lankan Sign Language (SSL). Existing assistive technologies are predominantly unidirectional, lack real-time capability, neglect emotional and prosodic nuance, and provide no environmental safety awareness for Deaf users.

This research addresses these gaps through an integrated transformer-based AI system comprising four interconnected components: (1) a Sign Language Recognition and Speech Output module using spatial-temporal transformers with adaptive on-device personalization; (2) a Speech-to-Sign Linguistic and Prosodic Processing module featuring a unified emotion-conditioned mT5 transformer with dual output heads generating SSL glosses and 3D bone rotations; (3) an AI-Powered Sound Awareness and Emergency SOS system utilizing a compressed CNN-Transformer classifier with TDOA localization; and (4) an Interactive Sign Language Learning and Practice module providing real-time gesture feedback and immersive gamified simulations.

The methodology employs Design Science Research with experimental validation. Data collection involves structured sessions at The School for the Deaf, Ratmalana. Principal integrated results demonstrate ≥90% recognition accuracy for isolated signs, ≤300ms end-to-end latency, ≥85% hazard sound detection accuracy, and ≥20% improvement in hearing users' signing accuracy. The research aligns with SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) by promoting social, economic, and political inclusion of persons with disabilities.

Keywords: Transformer Architecture, Sign Language Recognition, Bidirectional Communication, Sri Lankan Sign Language, Assistive Technology.

Solution Architecture

Four Integrated Components

Each component addresses a distinct sub-problem, working together as one unified system.

Component 1

Sign Language Recognition & Speech Output

Owner: Chamara R M L K · IT22076816

Converts real-time video of SSL signing into natural-sounding speech using a spatial-temporal transformer, MediaPipe Holistic (543 landmarks), co-articulation modeling, and adaptive on-device personalization via online reinforcement learning.

Component 2

Speech-to-Sign Linguistic & Prosodic Processing

Owner: Malkith G W L · IT22630834

Transforms Sinhala speech into expressive 3D avatar signing through a 6-stage pipeline: Whisper ASR → librosa prosody → sinling morphological normalization → XLM-RoBERTa emotion → unified mT5 transformer with dual heads (gloss + bone rotations).

Component 3

AI-Powered Sound Awareness & Emergency SOS

Owner: Gimhan T P K · IT22266996

Continuous environmental monitoring using a compressed CNN-Transformer classifier trained on localized Sri Lankan hazard sounds, TDOA directional localization, 360° radar visualization, priority-coded haptics, and autonomous GPS-tagged SOS dispatch within 5 seconds.

Component 4

Interactive Sign Language Learning & Practice

Owner: Ranathunga R A K N · IT22552860

Enables hearing users to learn SSL through real-time gesture feedback (using Component 1's recognition engine), personalized adaptive learning via error pattern analysis, and immersive gamified simulations of real-world scenarios.

Domain

Research Domain & Problem

Literature survey, research gap, problem statement, and objectives.

📚 Literature Survey

🎥

Transformer-Based Sign Recognition

SPOTER (Boháček & Hrůz, 2022) achieved 63.18% on WLASL-100 using pose landmarks. LAT (Guo et al., 2022) reached 98.27% BLEU-1 on CSL for translation. SLRFormer (Xiao et al., 2020) applied Vision Transformers to continuous signing.

🔊

Sound Awareness for DHH Users

SoundWatch (Jain et al., 2022) used MobileNetV2 on smartwatches. ProtoSound applied few-shot learning. SonoCraftAR used CNN-Transformer on AR headsets but required expensive hardware with only 2–3 hours battery life.

🇱🇰

Sri Lankan Sign Language Research

Hastha (Wanasinghe et al., 2022) provided SSL learning (91–100% game accuracy). Idushan et al. (2024) achieved 99.2% dynamic sign detection. Peiris (2019) reached 95% for static fingerspelling. All are learning-focused, not real-time communication systems.

🔍 Research Gap

Gap 1 — No Bidirectional Architecture

Every reviewed system operates in only one direction: either sign-to-text OR speech-to-text. No integrated system enables seamless two-way conversation within a single unified platform.

Gap 2 — No Real-Time End-to-End Pipeline

Saproo & Aggarwal (2024) explicitly state their system "is not capable of translating a given sign into a complete sentence." The <300ms latency required for natural conversation remains unachieved.

Gap 3 — Emotional & Prosodic Nuance Ignored

Sign language conveys emotion through facial expressions, body posture, and signing speed. Every existing system ignores this dimension entirely, producing flat, emotionless output.

Gap 4 — No Environmental Safety Awareness

Deaf individuals cannot hear alarms, approaching vehicles, or emergency cries. None of the 12 reviewed sources address this safety vulnerability.

Gap 5 — Severe SSL Under-Resourcing

SSL receives attention from only three research efforts. No commercial product supports SSL. Over 300,000 Deaf Sri Lankans remain underserved by assistive technology.

Gap 6 — Learning Disconnected from Communication

Existing learning platforms operate in isolation from authentic communication contexts. Learners cannot transition smoothly from practice to real conversation with Deaf individuals.

❓ Research Problem

The absence of a real-time, bidirectional communication system that enables natural, expressive interaction between Sri Lankan Sign Language users and hearing individuals, while simultaneously providing environmental safety awareness and supporting hearing individuals in learning SSL.

🎯 Research Objectives

Objective 1

Real-Time Sign Recognition & Speech Output

Achieve ≥90% recognition accuracy for isolated signs, ≥85% for continuous signing, and ≤300ms end-to-end latency with adaptive on-device personalization.

Objective 2

Emotion-Aware Speech-to-Sign Synthesis

Achieve ≥85% emotion classification accuracy, ≥80% Deaf user satisfaction with avatar expressiveness, and ≤300ms processing latency.

Objective 3

Environmental Sound Awareness & SOS

Achieve ≥85% hazard detection accuracy with ≤5% false positives, ±15° directional accuracy, SOS dispatch within 5 seconds, and ≤10% daily battery consumption.

Objective 4

Interactive SSL Learning Module

Demonstrate ≥20% improvement in hearing users' signing accuracy after 10 sessions, ≥4.0/5.0 user satisfaction, and ≥80% successful transition to real conversation.

Objective 5

System Integration & User Validation

Integrate all four components into a unified cross-platform application (Android & Windows), and conduct comprehensive user testing with at least 40 participants (20 Deaf, 20 hearing) from the Sri Lankan Deaf community, achieving ≥85% overall satisfaction.

Methodology

Research Approach & Technologies

🔬

Design Science Research (DSR)

This research adopts DSR combined with iterative prototyping and user-centered evaluation — a problem-solving paradigm that creates innovative IT artifacts to address real-world problems while generating new knowledge.

Five phases: (1) Problem Identification → (2) Objectives Definition → (3) Design & Development → (4) Demonstration → (5) Evaluation.

📊

Validation Strategy

Quantitative and qualitative metrics at component and system level, including paired t-tests and ANOVA for statistical significance, plus inter-rater reliability (Cohen's Kappa) for subjective ratings.

Evaluation via 40 participants (20 Deaf, 20 hearing) in three real-world scenarios: healthcare consultation, job interview, and emergency drill.

Key Findings

  • Bidirectional communication achieved: All 40 participants successfully completed conversations in both directions.
  • Latency targets met: End-to-end latency consistently below thresholds across all components.
  • Safety features valued: Deaf participants rated sound awareness as the most valuable feature.
  • Learning effectiveness confirmed: Hearing participants showed significant skill improvement after 10 sessions.
  • Integration success: No critical failures observed during end-to-end system testing.
